The Controllers' Journey: From Egypt to Rome, From Rome to Modern Times

Knowledge Transfer — A Continuous Chain

Ancient control systems lasted for thousands of years. When one civilization fell, the control knowledge did not perish — it was transferred to another civilization. This is a continuous chain, like a baton passed from generation to generation, from civilization to civilization.

Sumeria → Egypt

The control knowledge that began in Sumeria around 3500 BCE — astronomy, mathematics, writing, and economics — traveled to the banks of the Nile. Egyptian priests received this knowledge and shaped it into religious control. Mystery schools, step-by-step knowledge transfer, initiation processes — all were perfected in Egypt.

Egypt → Greece

Greek philosophers like Pythagoras trained in Egyptian Mystery Schools. They brought this knowledge to Greece and shaped it into philosophy and controlled ideologies. Plato's Allegory of the Cave openly described this control system.

From Egypt to Rome — The Control Center Shifts

From Akhenaten to Moses — The Journey of Monotheism

The revolution Akhenaten created — "one god, formless, no mediator needed" — this idea escaped from Egypt. Moses took this idea, parted the Red Sea, and traveled from Egypt to Canaan. From there, monotheism, Abrahamic faith, and Judaism were born.

Judaism → Christianity → Islam — The Evolution of Religions

When the Roman Empire conquered Egypt, the controllers moved into Rome. They controlled Rome. When Rome weakened and Judaism stood against Rome, when the Jews stood — the controllers brought division among the Jews too. From there, a new religion was born — Christianity.

As Christianity gradually became more powerful, they took the Roman Empire and merged it into Christianity. This happened in the fourth century, under Emperor Constantine. Then the Vatican system began. Later, Islam was born. The controllers infiltrated all three of these religions too.

This is the Controlled Opposition strategy. The forces born from Akhenaten's revolution — the monotheistic religions — were taken over by the controllers. Even religions born as opposition forces were infiltrated.

The Controllers — No Loyalty to Names, Places, Languages, or Religions

These controllers have no connection to names, places, languages, or religions. Whatever system exists, they easily enter it, adapt, and control it. If a revolution comes against them, they conveniently join that revolution and merge into it.

The controllers are never in one place. They were priests in Egypt. In Rome, they became emperors' advisors. In the Vatican, they became Cardinals. In Europe, they became bankers. In America, they became financial executives. Names change, languages change, countries change — but their purpose remains one: control.

Rome → Europe — The Birth of Vatican

After the Roman Empire fell, the Catholic Church seized this control knowledge. The Vatican became the spiritual successor of the Roman Empire — using faith instead of laws, religion instead of armies. When Emperor Constantine merged Christianity into the Roman Empire in the fourth century, the controllers continued Roman authority under the name of Christianity.

The Vatican is not merely a religious institution. It is the modern form of Egyptian priest knowledge. Concentrating secret knowledge in a few hands, providing prescribed beliefs to the masses, and controlling through faith — all methods that came directly from Egypt. The Vatican library holds some of the world's most ancient and secret documents — but only a few have access to see them.

Europe → The World — The Columbus Era

From the Columbus era, European powers spread these control systems across the world. Today, through globalization, these systems have penetrated every country, every society. The control system that began in Egypt, went through Rome, through the Vatican, to London, from London to New York — continues even today.

The Modern Picture

Today, the same systems appear in modern form. Names have changed, technology has changed — but the fundamental principle of control has not:

Vatican: The modern form of Egyptian priest knowledge. Concentrating secret knowledge in a few hands, providing prescribed beliefs to the masses, and controlling through faith.

Central Banks: The modern form of Babylonian temple banks. Federal Reserve, ECB, RBI — they all work just like ancient Babylonian temples. Giving loans, determining interest rates, and controlling the economy. The only difference — then the temple was built with mud bricks, now it is built with glass and steel.

Media: Ideologies controlled through Greek philosophy. Today's news channels, social media platforms, and entertainment industry are all modern tools that display Plato's cave shadows. People believe they are "informed" — but what they see is carefully selected, directed information. Left, right, center — people immersed in debates among these three ideologies forget who is providing their information.

Every protest movement, every political party, every organization — contains controlled opposition. People think "we are fighting against the system" — but the very war they fight is also run by the controllers.

Key Takeaways

Three important lessons emerge from this 5,000-year history of control:

1. Knowledge is always in the control of a few. From Sumerian priests to today's central bankers, the fundamental principle of control systems is one: concentrate knowledge and control its flow.

2. Control systems adapt to every era. When religion became outdated, money came. When money was not enough, ideology came. When ideology was not enough, controlled opposition came. The control system always appears in new form — but its purpose remains one.

3. People are always under some form of control — only the form changes. From Pharaoh to Federal Reserve, from Ziggurat to smartphone screen — control tools have changed, but the reality of control has not.

Conclusion — and the Next Chapter

This story begins in Sumeria. It is perfected in Egypt. Akhenaten challenges it. In Babylon, control transforms through debt. In Greece, control becomes more sophisticated through ideology. In Rome, it spreads through law and military. In the Vatican, it continues through faith. In London through banking, in New York through finance — to this day.

But this story does not end here. The Roman Empire may appear to have fallen, but in reality, it did not fall. It merely changed form. The Roman Empire still controls the world. That story — how the Roman Empire still controls the world — is in the next chapter.
